HYUNGRYUL HARRY BAIK / RESEARCH

Research

I work in low-dimensional topology and geometric group theory. The questions I return to most often concern how geometry, dynamics, and algebra constrain one another: when a group action reveals an underlying geometric structure, how the dynamics of a surface map reflect its algebraic properties, and how much of a group survives passage to its large-scale geometry. More recently, I have also been working on mathematics and AI, both on evaluating mathematical reasoning and on using topology to learn from data.

Dynamics, laminations, and low-dimensional geometry

A guiding question in this part of my work, going back to my doctoral thesis, is how much geometry can be recovered from an action on a circle. A circle lamination records a collection of non-crossing geodesics in a disk by their endpoints, and invariant laminations can distinguish circle actions that come from hyperbolic geometry from those that do not. With Kyeongro Kim, I proved that a subgroup of orientation-preserving circle homeomorphisms preserving three quite-full laminations with pairwise disjoint endpoint sets is conjugate to a Fuchsian group, that is, a discrete group of isometries of the hyperbolic plane. This removes the discreteness and torsion-free assumptions from my earlier characterization.

The three-dimensional analogue of this question leads to pairs of laminations. With Hongtaek Jung and Kyeongro Kim, I showed that groups preserving a veering pair arise as fundamental groups of irreducible 3-orbifolds, and that under a further cofiniteness condition on the action these orbifolds are hyperbolic. In a preprint with Chenxi Wu and Bojun Zhao, we reconstruct manifolds and flows from suitable circle actions with invariant almost laminations; the construction covers all orientable transitive pseudo-Anosov flows in closed 3-manifolds. Throughout, the aim is the same: to build the geometry out of the dynamics rather than assume a manifold from the start.

Selected papers:

  • Fuchsian Groups, Circularly Ordered Groups, and Dense Invariant Laminations on the Circle. Geometry & Topology (2015).
  • Characterization of Fuchsian Groups as Laminar Groups & the Structure Theorem of Hyperbolic 2-Orbifolds, with K. Kim. Accepted, to appear in Glasnik Matematicki.
  • Groups acting on veering pairs and Kleinian groups, with H. Jung and K. Kim. Journal of the London Mathematical Society (2025).
  • Reconstruction of Anosov flows from infinity, with C. Wu and B. Zhao. Preprint.

Mapping class groups and the geometry of surfaces

Here I ask what quantitative measurements of a surface map reveal about its algebra and its mapping torus. A central such measurement is the translation length, which records the average distance a map moves points under iteration, particularly on the curve graph. My joint work gives bounds on these lengths for pseudo-Anosov maps under constraints on their action on homology, and for the monodromies of different fibrations of a fixed hyperbolic 3-manifold. These questions turn out to be connected to normal generation—whether a mapping class together with its conjugates generates the whole mapping class group. For a closed hyperbolic fibered 3-manifold, we prove that all but finitely many primitive integral classes in any two-dimensional rational slice of a fibered cone have normally generating monodromies.

A related question asks how much of a hyperbolic surface is determined by the lengths of its simple closed geodesics when one does not know which curve carries which length. With Inhyeok Choi and Dongryul M. Kim, I proved rigidity results for length identities and used them to show that the unmarked simple length spectrum determines a generic orientable Nielsen-convex hyperbolic surface up to isometry, including surfaces of infinite type. Here "generic" means outside a meagre exceptional set in Teichmüller space; the result is not a rigidity statement for every surface.

Selected papers:

  • Asymptotic translation lengths and normal generation for pseudo-Anosov monodromies of fibered 3-manifolds, with E. Kin, H. Shin, and C. Wu. Algebraic & Geometric Topology (2023).
  • Minimal asymptotic translation lengths on curve complexes and homology of mapping tori, with D. M. Kim and C. Wu. Michigan Mathematical Journal (2025).
  • Simple length spectra as moduli for hyperbolic surfaces and rigidity of length identities, with I. Choi and D. M. Kim. Accepted, to appear in Annales de l'Institut Fourier.

Geometric group theory and group actions

The common thread in this part of my work is what an action can detect about a group, and what information it loses. A long-standing theme is smoothability: whether a topological action can be realized by differentiable maps. With Sang-hyun Kim and Thomas Koberda, I proved that no finite-index subgroup of the mapping class group of an orientable surface of complexity 3g − 3 + n + b ≥ 2 admits a faithful action on the circle or closed interval by orientation-preserving C¹ diffeomorphisms whose derivatives have bounded variation. Here g, n, and b denote the genus, the number of marked points, and the number of boundary components. The obstruction comes from a right-angled Artin group, so the theorem illustrates how algebraic relations can prevent smooth actions.

Other projects study actions at large scales and at infinity. With Inhyeok Choi and Dongryul M. Kim, I proved at least linear growth of translation lengths for non-elementary random walks on isometry groups of Gromov hyperbolic spaces, without any moment assumption. With Won Yong Jang, I identified the kernel of the natural action of a finitely generated acylindrically hyperbolic group on every asymptotic cone as its maximal finite normal subgroup, and related these kernels to actions on boundaries. Our preprint on profinite completions takes a complementary view: we construct two finitely generated residually finite groups with isomorphic profinite completions of which only one is co-Hopfian, meaning that only one has the property that every injective endomorphism is surjective.

Selected papers:

  • Unsmoothable group actions on compact one-manifolds, with S. Kim and T. Koberda. Journal of the European Mathematical Society (2019).
  • Linear growth of translation lengths of random isometries on Gromov hyperbolic spaces and Teichmüller spaces, with I. Choi and D. M. Kim. Journal of the Institute of Mathematics of Jussieu (2024).
  • On the kernel of actions on asymptotic cones, with W. Jang. Journal of Group Theory (2025).
  • Co-Hopfianity is not a profinite property, with W. Jang. Preprint.

Mathematics and AI

My interest here runs in two directions: how to evaluate mathematical reasoning, and how mathematical structure can improve learning. On the evaluation side, SH² is a mathematician-curated benchmark for research-level mathematical reasoning. Beyond posing challenging problems, it tests whether models recognize ill-posed questions instead of producing unjustified answers. I also participate as a coauthor in Lean-GAP, a dataset of graduate algebra problems formalized in Lean 4; the emphasis there is not only on producing formal statements but on checking that they faithfully express the original mathematics.

In the other direction, my work with Namwoo Kim and Yoonjin Yoon uses topology to learn representations of time series. TopoCL encodes persistence diagrams derived from persistent homology and jointly trains temporal contrastive learning and time–topology correspondence, with the goal of retaining structural information that ordinary time-series augmentations can distort.

Selected papers:

  • SH²: A Mathematician-Curated benchmark for Assessing Research-level Math Capabilities of LLMs, with G. Son et al. Accepted at NeurIPS 2026, Evaluations & Datasets Track (poster). The linked preprint is titled Soohak.
  • Lean-GAP: A Dataset of Formalized Graduate Algebra Problems, with S. Lee et al. Preprint.
  • TopoCL: Topological Contrastive Learning for Time Series, with N. Kim and Y. Yoon. IEEE Transactions on Neural Networks and Learning Systems (2026).
